# Policy 320 — Domestic Violence

> Kestrel County Sheriff's Office Policy Manual · Effective 2023-11-01 · Revised 2026-04-08
> FICTIONAL POLICY FOR DEMONSTRATION PURPOSES ONLY

## 320.1 Purpose and Scope
Domestic violence causes serious physical and emotional harm, often escalates over time, and is a leading factor in homicides and in assaults on law enforcement officers. This policy directs how members of the Kestrel County Sheriff's Office respond to, investigate, and document domestic violence incidents. The Office's priorities are the safety of victims and children, holding offenders accountable, and connecting victims with support services. Domestic violence shall be treated as a crime and not as a private family matter.

## 320.2 Definitions
**Domestic violence** — Abuse, assault, threats, stalking, or other criminal conduct committed against a family or household member or a current or former intimate partner, as defined by applicable state law.

**Family or household member** — Spouses, former spouses, persons who share a child, persons who live or have lived together, persons in a dating relationship, and other relationships recognized by applicable state law.

**Primary aggressor** — The person determined to be the most significant aggressor in an incident, rather than simply the first person to use force.

**Protective order** — Any court order restraining a person from contacting, threatening, or approaching another person, including emergency, temporary, and permanent orders issued by any court.

**Lethality assessment** — A short, structured screening tool used to identify victims at elevated risk of being seriously injured or killed by an intimate partner.

## 320.3 Officer Safety and Response
Domestic violence calls are unpredictable. Deputies should be dispatched in pairs whenever possible and should approach with caution, observing the location before contact. Dispatch shall check the address and involved parties for prior calls, protective orders, warrants, and known weapons, and shall broadcast that information to responding deputies.

Repeat addresses are flagged in the computer-aided dispatch system. Deputies responding to a flagged address should review the prior call history while en route and be aware that prior incidents, threats, or weapons at the residence may increase the danger. Upon arrival, deputies shall separate the parties, ensure that all persons in the home are accounted for, and check on the welfare of any children.

## 320.4 Investigation
Deputies shall conduct a thorough investigation regardless of whether the victim wishes to pursue charges, and regardless of the relationship, gender, or status of the parties. The investigation should include:

- Separate interviews of the involved parties and any witnesses, including children when appropriate and done sensitively.
- Photographs of injuries, the scene, and damaged property, with follow-up photographs when bruising may develop.
- Documentation of the victim's statements, demeanor, and signs of strangulation, such as voice changes, difficulty swallowing, or marks on the neck.
- Collection of 911 recordings, text messages, and other evidence.
- A check for existing protective orders and the suspect's criminal history.

Body-worn cameras shall be activated as required by Policy 342. A victim's reluctance or recantation does not end the investigation.

## 320.5 Arrest Guidelines
When a deputy has probable cause to believe that a domestic violence offense has occurred, the deputy shall make an arrest when required by applicable state law. When arrest is discretionary, arrest is the preferred response if the offense involved injury, strangulation, a weapon, a protective order violation, or a credible threat of future violence.

A deputy shall not base an arrest decision on the victim's wishes, the presence of children, the parties' marital status, the suspect's occupation, or speculation that an arrest will not lead to prosecution. Deputies shall not threaten to arrest both parties to discourage a report. When a deputy does not arrest despite probable cause, the reasons shall be documented and approved by a supervisor.

## 320.6 Primary Aggressor Determination
When both parties have used force, deputies shall attempt to determine the primary aggressor rather than arresting both parties. Relevant considerations include:

- The relative severity of the injuries and whether any injuries are defensive.
- The history of domestic violence between the parties.
- Statements of witnesses and the likelihood of future injury to each party.
- The relative size and strength of the parties and any fear expressed.
- Whether either party acted in self-defense.

Dual arrests are discouraged and require supervisor approval and a documented explanation of why each party was found to be an aggressor.

## 320.7 Lethality Assessment
Deputies shall complete the Office's lethality assessment with every victim of intimate partner violence who is willing to participate. Questions address factors such as prior threats to kill, access to firearms, prior strangulation, escalating violence, and recent separation. When the screening indicates high danger, the deputy shall advise the victim of the risk, immediately offer to connect the victim by phone with the Kestrel County Domestic Violence Advocacy Program, and notify a supervisor. The completed screening shall be attached to the report.

## 320.8 Firearms Considerations
Deputies shall ask both parties whether firearms are present in the home or accessible to the suspect. Firearms in plain view, used in the incident, or that pose a risk may be seized for safekeeping or as evidence consistent with Policy 322 (Search and Seizure). Deputies should be aware that federal law and applicable state law may prohibit firearm possession by persons subject to qualifying protective orders or convicted of qualifying domestic violence offenses, and shall document any such prohibition in the report. Seized firearms shall be handled under Policy 802.

## 320.9 Victim Assistance
Deputies shall provide every victim with the Office's written victim rights and resources brochure, including the case number, contact information for the investigating deputy, shelter and hotline numbers, and information on how to obtain a protective order. Deputies should help arrange transportation to a safe location or medical facility when needed and remain at the scene while the victim gathers essential belongings if safety concerns exist. Victim addresses shall be protected from public release as described in Policy 810.

## 320.10 Protective Orders
Deputies shall enforce valid protective orders from any jurisdiction as required by applicable state law and federal full faith and credit provisions. When service of an emergency protective order is authorized, deputies shall serve it on the respondent and document the service. A respondent's violation of a protective order shall be investigated as a criminal offense.

## 320.11 Reports and Follow-Up
A written report shall be completed for every domestic violence call, even when no arrest is made or no crime is found. Reports shall be completed before the end of shift and shall identify the call as domestic-related. The Investigations Division shall review domestic violence reports for follow-up, including cases involving high lethality scores or repeat victimization.
