# Policy 830 — Generative AI and Decision-Support Tools

> Kestrel County Sheriff's Office Policy Manual · Effective 2026-01-01 · Revised 2026-09-01
> FICTIONAL POLICY FOR DEMONSTRATION PURPOSES ONLY

## 830.1 Purpose and Scope
Generative artificial intelligence (AI) and other decision-support tools can help members of the Kestrel County Sheriff's Office find information, summarize records, and draft documents more efficiently. These tools can also produce inaccurate, incomplete, or biased output, and they raise concerns about privacy, data security, and due process. This policy establishes the conditions under which members may use these tools, the safeguards that apply, and the uses that are prohibited.

This policy applies to all members, contractors, and volunteers, and to any AI or decision-support tool used for Office business, including policy question-and-answer assistants, report drafting aids, transcription and summarization tools, and analytical tools. It works together with Policy 602 (Report Preparation) and Policy 820 (Criminal Justice Information Security).

## 830.2 Definitions
**Generative AI** — Software that produces text, images, audio, or other content in response to prompts, typically using a large language model.

**Decision-support tool** — Any software that analyzes information and offers recommendations, summaries, rankings, or answers intended to assist a human decision.

**Approved tool** — An AI or decision-support system that has been reviewed and authorized in writing by the AI Governance Committee and listed on the Office's approved tools register.

**Agency-controlled system** — A system hosted on Office or County infrastructure, or under a contract that meets the FBI CJIS Security Policy, where the Office controls data storage, access, and retention.

**Output** — Any answer, draft, summary, citation, or recommendation produced by a tool.

## 830.3 Approved Tools Only
Members shall use only approved tools for Office business. Approved tools that process criminal justice information, personal information, or case information must be agency-controlled systems, either running on-premises or in an environment that meets the FBI CJIS Security Policy, and shall not send prompts or data to outside services for training or retention.

Members shall not enter CJI, case details, personal identifying information, victim information, or any non-public Office information into public or consumer AI services, including free chatbots and browser extensions. Using a consumer AI service for general, non-sensitive tasks, such as drafting a training announcement, is permitted only when no Office or personal data is included.

## 830.4 Output Is Advisory
AI output is advisory and may be wrong. Members remain fully responsible for every decision they make and every document they sign. AI output shall never be the sole basis for:

- A finding of reasonable suspicion or probable cause.
- An arrest, search, seizure, or warrant application.
- A charging recommendation or case closure.
- A use of force decision or a determination that force was within policy.
- Discipline, hiring, or other personnel decisions.

Any fact, lead, or conclusion suggested by an AI tool must be independently verified against original records or personal knowledge before it is relied upon.

## 830.5 Citations and Verification
Approved tools used to answer questions about policy, law, or records shall provide citations to the specific source, such as a policy section number or record identifier. Members shall verify any answer they rely on by reading the cited source. An answer without a citation, or one whose citation does not support it, shall not be relied upon. Members who identify an inaccurate answer or citation should report it through the tool's feedback function or to the AI Governance Committee.

## 830.6 AI-Assisted Reports
Members may use approved tools to help draft reports only as allowed by Policy 602. The deputy is the author of the report and attests that it is accurate and complete. The deputy shall review the entire draft, correct errors, and remove any statement not supported by the deputy's own observations or the evidence.

Every report prepared with AI assistance shall include the following disclosure at the end of the narrative: "Portions of this report were drafted with the assistance of an Office-approved AI tool. I have reviewed the entire report and attest that it is accurate." A deputy's account of their own perceptions, decisions, and reasons for using force shall be written in the deputy's own words.

## 830.7 Logging and Auditability
All approved tools shall log each prompt, output, user identity, date, and time, and these logs shall be retained according to the Office retention schedule and be available for audit by the Professional Standards Unit and the AI Governance Committee. Members have no expectation of privacy in their use of approved tools. Members shall not attempt to disable logging, delete history, or use tools in a way designed to avoid review.

## 830.8 Prohibited Uses
Members shall not use AI or decision-support tools to:

- Generate predictive risk scores or forecasts of criminal behavior for specific individuals.
- Perform facial recognition or biometric identification, except as authorized under a separate Office policy governing that technology.
- Profile, target, or prioritize people based on race, ethnicity, religion, national origin, gender, sexual orientation, disability, or other protected characteristics.
- Create or alter images, audio, or video presented as evidence.
- Conduct surveillance of lawful First Amendment activity.
- Generate content intended to deceive the public, courts, or other agencies.

## 830.9 Discovery and Brady Considerations
AI-assisted drafts, prompts, outputs, and logs related to a case may be discoverable and may contain exculpatory or impeachment information. These materials shall be preserved according to the retention schedule and shall not be deleted while a case is pending. Members and investigators shall disclose the use of AI tools to the prosecuting attorney when asked and in any case where AI output contributed to the investigation, consistent with Policy 600.

## 830.10 Training and Access
Members shall complete Office training on approved tools before receiving access. Training shall cover the limitations of AI, verification of output, data security, prohibited uses, disclosure requirements, and recognizing bias. Access shall be renewed annually upon completion of refresher training.

## 830.11 Governance, Accuracy, and Bias Review
The AI Governance Committee, chaired by the Undersheriff and including representatives from the Information Technology Unit, the Professional Standards Unit, the Records Unit, and County Counsel, shall approve tools before use and maintain the approved tools register. The Committee shall review each approved tool at least twice per year for accuracy, citation reliability, and evidence of bias, using test questions and a sample of logged queries. Tools that fail review shall be suspended until corrected. The Committee shall publish an annual summary of the Office's AI use.
