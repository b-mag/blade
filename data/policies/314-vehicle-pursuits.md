# Policy 314 — Vehicle Pursuits

> Kestrel County Sheriff's Office Policy Manual · Effective 2024-04-01 · Revised 2026-01-12
> FICTIONAL POLICY FOR DEMONSTRATION PURPOSES ONLY

## 314.1 Purpose and Scope
Vehicle pursuits present a serious danger to deputies, the person being pursued, and members of the public who are not involved. This policy establishes when deputies of the Kestrel County Sheriff's Office may initiate or continue a pursuit, the responsibilities of deputies and supervisors, and the circumstances that require a pursuit to end. The Office's guiding principle is that the need to immediately apprehend a suspect must clearly outweigh the risks created by the pursuit.

## 314.2 Definitions
**Vehicle pursuit** — An active attempt by a deputy in a marked or authorized emergency vehicle to apprehend the driver of a vehicle who is attempting to avoid apprehension by maintaining or increasing speed, ignoring emergency signals, or taking evasive action.

**Primary unit** — The Office vehicle that initiates the pursuit or that is closest to the suspect vehicle.

**Secondary unit** — The Office vehicle assigned to support the primary unit and take over communications.

**Pursuit intervention technique (PIT)** — A trained maneuver in which a deputy uses the patrol vehicle to cause the suspect vehicle to spin out and stop.

**Termination** — The decision to discontinue a pursuit, after which all units shut down emergency equipment, reduce speed, and resume normal driving.

## 314.3 Initiating a Pursuit
A deputy may initiate a pursuit only when the deputy has reasonable suspicion that the driver or an occupant has committed or is attempting to commit a violent felony, or a felony involving the threatened or actual use of a weapon, or when the driver's conduct before the attempt to stop presents a clear and immediate danger to others, such as a suspected impaired driver operating in a way likely to cause a collision.

Pursuits shall not be initiated for:

- Minor traffic violations or infractions.
- Misdemeanor offenses.
- Property crimes alone, including vehicle theft, unless additional factors meet the criteria above.

When a vehicle fails to stop for a lower-level offense, deputies should document the vehicle and plate information and pursue follow-up investigation rather than giving chase.

## 314.4 Factors to Consider
Before and throughout a pursuit, deputies and supervisors shall continuously weigh:

- The seriousness of the offense and the known identity of the suspect.
- Traffic volume, pedestrian presence, and whether schools or events are in session.
- Weather, lighting, and road conditions.
- Speeds involved and the driving behavior of the suspect.
- The deputy's training, familiarity with the area, and vehicle condition.
- Whether passengers, hostages, or juveniles are in either vehicle.
- The availability of air support or other means to locate the suspect later.

## 314.5 Deputy Responsibilities
Upon initiating a pursuit, the primary unit shall activate emergency lights and siren and immediately notify dispatch of the location, direction, speed, vehicle description, number of occupants, and the reason for the pursuit. The primary unit shall continue to drive with due regard for the safety of all persons and shall not exceed speeds that are unsafe for the conditions.

Deputies shall not pursue against the flow of traffic on divided highways, shall not drive alongside the suspect vehicle, and shall not discharge firearms at or from a moving vehicle except as permitted by Section 300.4.3 of Policy 300. Unmarked vehicles and vehicles carrying civilians or prisoners shall not participate in pursuits.

## 314.6 Number of Units
No more than two Office units shall actively engage in a pursuit unless a supervisor authorizes additional units based on the nature of the offense, the number of suspects, or other clear need. Other units should remain aware of the pursuit's direction and position themselves to assist at the termination point without joining the pursuit or paralleling it at high speed. Air support, when available, should take over tracking so ground units can reduce speed and distance.

## 314.7 Supervisor Control
A field supervisor shall acknowledge the pursuit over the radio and assume control as soon as practical. The supervisor is not required to join the pursuit physically. The supervisor shall monitor the pursuit, ensure that this policy is followed, approve or deny the use of intervention techniques, and order termination when the risks outweigh the need for immediate apprehension.

Any supervisor or watch commander may terminate a pursuit at any time. When no supervisor is available, the primary deputy is responsible for compliance with this policy.

## 314.8 Termination of a Pursuit
A pursuit shall be terminated when:

- A supervisor orders it to end.
- The danger to the public or deputies outweighs the need for immediate apprehension.
- The suspect's identity is known and apprehension can occur later.
- Radio communication is lost or the suspect vehicle's location is unknown.
- The deputy's vehicle develops a mechanical problem or emergency equipment fails.
- The pursuit enters an area of heavy pedestrian traffic or a school zone during school hours.

Deputies will not be disciplined for good-faith decisions to terminate a pursuit.

## 314.9 Intervention Techniques
PIT maneuvers may be used only by deputies trained and certified in the technique and only with supervisor approval, unless exigent circumstances make obtaining approval impractical. PIT shall not be used at speeds above the limit established in training, on vehicles carrying hazardous materials, on motorcycles, or where the suspect vehicle could strike pedestrians or roll into a hazardous area.

Tire deflation devices may be deployed by trained deputies at locations that allow safe deployment and retreat. Roadblocks and intentional ramming are considered deadly force and may be used only when deadly force would be authorized under Policy 300.

## 314.10 Interjurisdictional Pursuits
When a pursuit is likely to enter another jurisdiction, dispatch shall notify that agency and provide pursuit details. KCSO deputies shall not take part in another agency's pursuit unless requested and approved by a KCSO supervisor, and then only if the pursuit meets the initiation criteria of this policy. When another agency takes over the pursuit, KCSO units should disengage unless asked to assist.

## 314.11 Reporting and Review
The primary deputy shall complete a pursuit report before the end of shift. The supervisor shall review the pursuit, including radio and video recordings, and forward a written critique through the chain of command within five days. The Training Unit shall conduct an annual analysis of all pursuits to identify trends and training needs.
