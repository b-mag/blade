# Policy 342 — Body-Worn Cameras

> Kestrel County Sheriff's Office Policy Manual · Effective 2023-08-01 · Revised 2025-12-05
> FICTIONAL POLICY FOR DEMONSTRATION PURPOSES ONLY

## 342.1 Purpose and Scope
Body-worn cameras (BWCs) provide an objective record of interactions between members of the Kestrel County Sheriff's Office and the public. They support transparency, accountability, evidence collection, and training. This policy establishes when BWCs must be activated, when recording is prohibited, how deputies may review footage, and how recordings are retained and released. It applies to all members issued a BWC, including deputies assigned to patrol, investigations, and the Kestrel County Detention Center when so equipped.

## 342.2 Definitions
**Body-worn camera (BWC)** — An Office-issued audio and video recording device worn on a member's uniform or body.

**Activation** — Placing the BWC in event mode so that it records and preserves audio and video, including the pre-event buffer.

**Pre-event buffer** — The period of video, without audio, that the BWC retains before activation.

**Digital evidence management system (DEMS)** — The secure system used to upload, store, categorize, and audit BWC recordings.

**Law enforcement contact** — Any interaction with a member of the public for an investigative or enforcement purpose.

## 342.3 Issuance and Inspection
Deputies issued a BWC shall wear it on the front of the uniform in the position established by training whenever working in uniform or engaged in field enforcement. At the start of each shift, deputies shall test the BWC for proper function and battery charge and report any malfunction to a supervisor immediately. Only Office-issued BWCs may be used, and members shall not alter, disable, obstruct, or erase a BWC or its recordings.

## 342.4 Required Activation
Deputies shall activate their BWC before arriving at, or as soon as safely possible during, any of the following:

- Calls for service and self-initiated law enforcement contacts.
- Traffic and pedestrian stops.
- Vehicle pursuits and emergency driving.
- Arrests, detentions, searches, and frisks.
- Uses of force or situations in which force may reasonably be anticipated.
- Transports of persons in custody.
- Interviews of victims, witnesses, and suspects, subject to Section 342.6.
- Any contact that becomes adversarial.

If a deputy fails to activate the BWC, interrupts a recording, or the camera malfunctions, the deputy shall document the reason in the report and notify a supervisor.

## 342.5 Duration of Recording
Once activated, the BWC shall remain recording until the contact has fully ended, the deputy has left the scene, or a supervisor authorizes deactivation. Before deactivating, the deputy should state the reason on the recording. Deputies may mute or pause recording only for brief tactical or administrative conversations with other members that do not involve the public, and shall state the reason before doing so.

## 342.6 Discretionary Deactivation and Privacy
Deputies may use discretion to stop recording when a victim or witness requests it and the deputy determines that the value of a recording is outweighed by the person's privacy interest or willingness to provide information, such as a sexual assault victim or a confidential informant. Deputies should record the request before deactivating. Deputies should also consider privacy interests when recording inside residences, medical facilities, and places of worship, but shall continue recording during any enforcement action.

## 342.7 Prohibited Recording
BWCs shall not be used to record:

- Non-work-related activity or personal conversations.
- Other members in locker rooms, restrooms, or break areas, except during a law enforcement contact.
- Strip searches or medical examinations, except when required for evidence.
- Communications with legal counsel or privileged conversations.
- Encounters with undercover officers or confidential informants, unless the encounter is part of an enforcement action.
- People engaged in lawful First Amendment activity solely to identify them.

## 342.8 Review of Recordings
Deputies may review their own BWC recordings before completing reports, to ensure accuracy, except as limited below. When a deputy reviews footage before writing a report, the report shall note that the footage was reviewed. Reports shall describe the deputy's own perceptions at the time of the event, and deputies should explain any differences between their perception and the recording.

Following a deadly force incident or in-custody death, involved deputies shall not review footage until authorized by the lead investigator, who will determine whether a statement is taken before or after review. Supervisors may review recordings for complaint investigation, use of force review, and performance auditing. Members shall not copy, share, or record BWC footage with personal devices.

## 342.9 Categorizing and Uploading
Deputies shall upload recordings to the DEMS before the end of their shift, or as soon as possible when exceptional circumstances apply. Each recording shall be labeled with the case or incident number and an appropriate category, which determines the retention period.

## 342.10 Retention
Recordings are retained according to category and the records retention schedule required by applicable state law. At a minimum:

- Non-evidentiary recordings: 180 days.
- Recordings involving an arrest or citation: until final disposition of the case plus any appeal period.
- Use of force, pursuit, or complaint recordings: three years or until closure of any related investigation or litigation, whichever is later.
- Homicide and major felony recordings: permanent or as directed by the prosecuting attorney.

Recordings subject to a litigation hold shall not be deleted.

## 342.11 Release of Recordings
BWC recordings are records of the Office and shall be released only as provided by Policy 810 (Records Release and Public Records Requests) and applicable state law. Before release, recordings shall be reviewed and redacted to protect victims, juveniles, medical information, the interiors of private residences where appropriate, and criminal justice information. In critical incidents, the Sheriff may authorize release of recordings in the public interest after consultation with the prosecuting attorney.

## 342.12 Audits
The Professional Standards Unit shall conduct monthly random audits of BWC recordings to evaluate compliance with activation requirements and identify training needs. DEMS audit logs of all access to recordings shall be retained and reviewed periodically.
