# Policy 820 — Criminal Justice Information Security

> Kestrel County Sheriff's Office Policy Manual · Effective 2023-10-01 · Revised 2026-02-20
> FICTIONAL POLICY FOR DEMONSTRATION PURPOSES ONLY

## 820.1 Purpose and Scope
The Kestrel County Sheriff's Office accesses criminal justice information (CJI) through FBI CJIS systems, state systems, and its own records management and computer-aided dispatch systems. This policy establishes the requirements for protecting CJI from unauthorized access, use, and disclosure, consistent with the FBI CJIS Security Policy, applicable state law, and the Office's agreements with the state CJIS Systems Agency. It applies to all members, contractors, and vendors with access to CJI or to systems that store or process it.

## 820.2 Definitions
**Criminal justice information (CJI)** — Data necessary for law enforcement and criminal justice agencies to perform their mission, including biometric, identity history, person, property, and case data, and criminal history record information.

**Criminal history record information (CHRI)** — A subset of CJI consisting of records of arrests, charges, dispositions, and related information.

**Terminal Agency Coordinator (TAC)** — The member designated as the Office's point of contact with the state CJIS Systems Agency and responsible for local compliance.

**Advanced authentication** — Authentication that requires more than a password, such as a hardware token, one-time code, or biometric, as required by the FBI CJIS Security Policy.

**Secondary dissemination** — The release of CJI by an authorized recipient to another person or agency.

## 820.3 Authorized Access
Access to CJI is limited to members who have completed a fingerprint-based background check, signed the Office's security acknowledgment, and completed CJIS security awareness training at the level appropriate to their role. Access is granted by the TAC based on the member's assignment and is removed promptly when a member separates, changes assignment, or is placed on administrative leave. Vendors and contractors require a signed CJIS Security Addendum and must meet the same screening standards before accessing CJI or unescorted access to secure areas.

## 820.4 Need-to-Know and Right-to-Know
Members may query, view, or use CJI only when they have both a right-to-know, meaning they are authorized by law and Office role, and a need-to-know, meaning the information is required for a specific, legitimate criminal justice purpose. Each query shall include an accurate reason code or case number. Curiosity, personal interest, and assisting friends, relatives, or outside parties are never valid purposes. Members shall not run queries on themselves, family members, acquaintances, celebrities, or public officials except as part of an assigned duty.

## 820.5 Physical and Technical Safeguards
CJI shall be accessed only from Office-approved devices and secure locations. Members shall:

- Lock or log off workstations and mobile data computers when unattended.
- Position screens to prevent viewing by unauthorized persons.
- Store printed CJI in secure areas and shred it when no longer needed.
- Never store CJI on personal devices, removable media not issued by the Office, or unapproved cloud services.
- Never enter CJI into public or consumer generative AI services, consistent with Policy 830.

Remote access and mobile access to CJI shall use advanced authentication and encryption that meet the FBI CJIS Security Policy.

## 820.6 Passwords and Advanced Authentication
Members shall protect their credentials and shall not share passwords, tokens, or accounts with any other person. Passwords shall meet the length and complexity requirements set by the Information Technology Unit. Advanced authentication is required for all access to CJI from outside a physically secure location, including from patrol vehicles. Lost or compromised tokens or credentials shall be reported immediately to the Information Technology Unit and the TAC.

## 820.7 Audit Logging
All systems that store or process CJI shall log user activity, including logons, queries, and record access, with the user identity, date, time, and purpose. Logs shall be retained for at least the period required by the FBI CJIS Security Policy. The TAC shall review query logs at least monthly and conduct targeted audits when misuse is suspected. Members should understand that all CJI activity is recorded and subject to review.

## 820.8 Dissemination
CJI may be shared only with authorized criminal justice agencies and persons for authorized purposes, and only as permitted by law. Each secondary dissemination of CHRI shall be recorded in a dissemination log identifying the date, the recipient, the information released, and the purpose. Members shall not release CJI to the public, media, employers, or private parties, and CJI shall be redacted from records released under Policy 810.

## 820.9 Security Incidents
Members who know or suspect that CJI has been lost, stolen, improperly accessed, or disclosed, or that a system containing CJI has been compromised, shall immediately notify a supervisor, the TAC, and the Information Technology Unit. The TAC shall report incidents to the state CJIS Systems Agency as required and coordinate the response.

## 820.10 Misuse and Discipline
Misuse of CJI, including unauthorized queries, disclosure, or use for personal purposes, is a serious violation of this policy and may result in discipline up to and including termination, loss of system access, and criminal prosecution under applicable state and federal law. The Office may be required to report misuse to the state CJIS Systems Agency.

## 820.11 Training
All members with access to CJI shall complete CJIS security awareness training within six months of assignment and before gaining access, and at least annually thereafter, at the level required by their role.
