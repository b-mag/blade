# Policy 324 — Juveniles

> Kestrel County Sheriff's Office Policy Manual · Effective 2024-07-01 · Revised 2026-03-15
> FICTIONAL POLICY FOR DEMONSTRATION PURPOSES ONLY

## 324.1 Purpose and Scope
This policy establishes procedures for members of the Kestrel County Sheriff's Office when taking juveniles into temporary custody, interviewing them, and handling records concerning them. The Office recognizes that juveniles are developmentally different from adults, are more susceptible to pressure during questioning, and have special protections under federal and applicable state law. The Office's approach emphasizes public safety, accountability, and diversion from the justice system when appropriate.

## 324.2 Definitions
**Juvenile** — A person under the age of 18, or as otherwise defined by applicable state law.

**Temporary custody** — The period during which a juvenile is held by the Office following an arrest or detention, before release to a parent or guardian or transfer to a juvenile facility.

**Non-secure custody** — Custody in which the juvenile is not locked in a room or cell or physically secured to a fixed object, although the juvenile remains under constant supervision.

**Secure custody** — Custody in which the juvenile is placed in a locked room or secured to a cuffing rail, permitted only for juveniles suspected of serious offenses and only as allowed by law.

**Status offender** — A juvenile alleged to have committed conduct that would not be a crime if committed by an adult, such as truancy or running away.

## 324.3 Taking Juveniles Into Custody
Deputies may take a juvenile into temporary custody when authorized by applicable state law, such as when the juvenile has committed a criminal offense, is the subject of a court order, or is in immediate danger. Deputies should consider alternatives to custody, including release to a parent with a citation, referral to a diversion program, or a warning, when the offense is minor and public safety does not require custody.

Juveniles who are ill, injured, intoxicated, or in a mental-health crisis shall receive medical evaluation before being held. Juveniles shall not be booked into the Kestrel County Detention Center unless authorized by court order or applicable state law for juveniles charged as adults.

## 324.4 Holding Juveniles at Office Facilities
Juveniles held at an Office facility shall be kept separated by sight and sound from adult prisoners. Status offenders and non-offenders, such as abused or neglected children, shall not be held in secure custody. Juveniles in non-secure custody shall be under constant visual supervision.

A juvenile shall be held at an Office facility only for the minimum time necessary to complete processing and arrange release or transfer, and in no case longer than the limit established by applicable state law. Deputies shall document the times of custody, any secure custody, meals, and the time of release or transfer on the juvenile custody log.

## 324.5 Parent or Guardian Notification
When a juvenile is taken into custody, the deputy shall make reasonable efforts to notify a parent, guardian, or responsible adult as soon as practical. The notification shall include the reason for custody and where the juvenile is being held. All notification attempts, successful or not, shall be documented. If no parent or guardian can be reached, the deputy shall notify a supervisor, who will determine whether to involve child protective services.

## 324.6 Interviews of Juveniles
Before a custodial interrogation of a juvenile, the deputy shall give Miranda warnings in age-appropriate language and confirm the juvenile understands them. Deputies shall comply with applicable state law regarding consultation with counsel or a parent before a waiver. When feasible, the parent or guardian should be notified before the interview and given the opportunity to be present, unless the parent is a suspect or victim in the matter or their presence would compromise the investigation.

Custodial interrogations of juveniles shall be recorded in their entirety. Interviews should be brief, conducted at reasonable hours, and free of deceptive tactics or threats. Juvenile victims of abuse or sexual assault should be referred to the Kestrel County Child Advocacy Center for forensic interviews.

## 324.7 School-Based Contacts
Deputies assigned to schools shall coordinate with school administrators but shall not be used to enforce routine school discipline. Interviews of students on school grounds about criminal matters should follow Section 324.6, and the deputy should inform school administration of the interview unless doing so would endanger the student or the investigation.

## 324.8 Juvenile Records
Juvenile records are confidential and have restricted access under applicable state law. Reports identifying a juvenile suspect shall be marked as juvenile records in the records management system, which limits access to members with a need-to-know and a right-to-know.

Members shall not release juvenile names, photographs, or identifying information to the media or the public, except as authorized by law or court order and approved by the Records Supervisor. Requests for juvenile records shall be handled under Policy 810. Juvenile fingerprints and photographs shall be taken and stored only as allowed by applicable state law, and records eligible for sealing or expungement shall be processed promptly upon court order.

## 324.9 Use of Force and Restraints
Force used on juveniles is governed by Policy 300, with deputies considering the juvenile's age, size, and maturity in assessing reasonableness. Restraints may be used under Policy 306. Conducted energy weapons should not be used on young children or on juveniles who are visibly small or frail except when deadly force would otherwise be justified.
