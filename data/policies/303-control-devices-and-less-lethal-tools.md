# Policy 303 — Control Devices and Less-Lethal Tools

> Kestrel County Sheriff's Office Policy Manual · Effective 2024-01-15 · Revised 2026-02-01
> FICTIONAL POLICY FOR DEMONSTRATION PURPOSES ONLY

## 303.1 Purpose and Scope
This policy governs the issuance, carrying, deployment, and documentation of control devices and less-lethal tools used by members of the Kestrel County Sheriff's Office. These tools are intended to give deputies options for controlling resistive or dangerous subjects while reducing the risk of serious injury to everyone involved. Their use is a use of force and is subject to all provisions of Policy 300 (Use of Force), including the objective reasonableness standard and the requirement to de-escalate when safe and feasible.

Members shall carry and use only devices issued or approved by the Office and only after completing the required training and qualification.

## 303.2 Definitions
**Control device** — An Office-approved tool designed to gain control of a subject with a reduced risk of serious injury, including oleoresin capsicum spray and batons.

**Conducted energy weapon (CEW)** — A device that uses electrical current to cause temporary neuromuscular incapacitation or pain compliance, either through probes fired from a distance or by direct contact (drive-stun).

**Impact munition** — A projectile, such as a 40mm sponge or foam round, fired from a dedicated launcher to deliver blunt force from a distance.

**Oleoresin capsicum (OC)** — An inflammatory agent derived from pepper plants, issued in aerosol form, that causes temporary irritation of the eyes, skin, and respiratory system.

**Vulnerable person** — A person who is visibly pregnant, elderly, a young child, frail, or known to have a heart condition or other condition that increases the risk of serious injury from a particular device.

## 303.3 General Deployment Standards
Before using any control device, deputies should, when feasible, give a warning that the device may be used and allow the subject a reasonable opportunity to comply. The warning should also alert other deputies that a device is about to be deployed.

Control devices shall not be used:

- Against a person who is only passively resisting, unless otherwise authorized in this policy.
- As punishment, to coerce statements, or in retaliation.
- Against a person who is handcuffed or secured, except to prevent imminent serious injury.
- In a manner that would foreseeably cause a fall from height or into water or traffic, unless deadly force would be justified.

Deputies shall consider the risk to vulnerable persons and bystanders, and shall reassess the subject's behavior after each application.

## 303.4 Oleoresin Capsicum Spray
OC spray may be used against subjects who are actively resisting, assaultive, or who present an imminent threat of physical harm to deputies or others. It should be directed toward the subject's face using short bursts from the distance recommended in training.

OC shall not be used in enclosed spaces where uninvolved persons cannot leave, near infants, or against crowds unless authorized by a supervisor for crowd control. After exposure, deputies shall provide decontamination as soon as practical, including flushing with cool water and fresh air, and shall monitor the subject for respiratory distress. A subject exposed to OC shall not be left in a prone position or unattended in a vehicle.

## 303.5 Conducted Energy Weapons
CEWs may be used when a subject is assaultive or when a subject is actively resisting in a manner that creates an immediate risk of injury to the subject, deputies, or others. A CEW shall not be used solely because a subject is fleeing on foot, and shall not be used on a handcuffed or restrained subject except to prevent imminent serious bodily injury that cannot be prevented by other means.

Deputies shall avoid targeting the head, neck, chest, and genitals, and should target the back or lower torso when feasible. Each energy cycle should be the standard five seconds or less, and the deputy shall reassess after each cycle. More than three cycles against a single subject, or simultaneous activation by more than one deputy, is prohibited unless deadly force would be authorized.

CEWs shall not be used near flammable materials, on a person in water, on a person operating a vehicle, or on a person at elevated height. Use against vulnerable persons requires a heightened justification. Drive-stun mode is used only to create distance and shall not be used solely for pain compliance.

## 303.6 40mm Impact Munitions
Only deputies trained and certified on the 40mm launcher may deploy impact munitions. The launcher may be used against a subject who is assaultive or who presents an immediate threat to deputies, the public, or themselves, including an armed subject in crisis where distance makes other less-lethal options ineffective.

Deputies should target large muscle groups such as the thighs and buttocks, and shall avoid the head, neck, spine, chest, and groin unless deadly force would be authorized. The deputy deploying the launcher should be paired with a lethal cover deputy and a designated arrest team. Deputies shall observe the minimum and maximum distances established in training and the manufacturer's guidance.

## 303.7 Batons and Impact Weapons
Batons may be used to block attacks, to strike when a subject is assaultive, and as a leverage tool in control techniques. Strikes should be directed to large muscle groups such as the arms and legs. Intentional strikes to the head, neck, throat, spine, kidneys, or groin are considered deadly force and are prohibited unless deadly force is authorized under Policy 300.

Improvised impact weapons, such as a flashlight or radio, may be used only in exigent circumstances when a baton is not available and shall be reported in the same manner as a baton strike.

## 303.8 Post-Deployment Medical Care
After any deployment of a control device that strikes or affects a subject, deputies shall:

- Provide or request medical care, and summon emergency medical services for any CEW probe strike, impact munition strike, or subject showing signs of distress.
- Not remove CEW probes from sensitive areas such as the face, neck, groin, or breast; probes in those areas shall be removed by medical personnel.
- Photograph injuries and probe sites when practical.
- Monitor the subject's breathing and consciousness until transferred to medical or detention staff.
- Notify Kestrel County Detention Center booking staff of the device used.

## 303.9 Reporting and Inspection
Every deployment of a control device, including a CEW arc display used as a warning, shall be reported to a supervisor and documented in a Use of Force report as required by Policy 300. The CEW's data log shall be downloaded by the supervisor or Training Unit within 72 hours and attached to the report.

Deputies shall inspect assigned devices at the start of each shift. Expired OC canisters, damaged batons, and malfunctioning CEWs shall be removed from service and reported to the Training Unit. Members shall complete annual recertification on each device they are authorized to carry.
