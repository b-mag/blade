# Policy 600 — Investigations and Case Management

> Kestrel County Sheriff's Office Policy Manual · Effective 2023-02-01 · Revised 2025-06-30
> FICTIONAL POLICY FOR DEMONSTRATION PURPOSES ONLY

## 600.1 Purpose and Scope
This policy establishes standards for conducting criminal investigations and managing cases within the Kestrel County Sheriff's Office. It covers the responsibilities of patrol deputies conducting preliminary investigations, the assignment and management of cases by the Investigations Division, and the standards for case documentation, follow-up, and closure. The goal is to ensure that investigations are thorough, objective, timely, and lawful, and that victims are kept informed.

## 600.2 Definitions
**Preliminary investigation** — The initial investigation conducted by the first responding deputy, including securing the scene, identifying witnesses and suspects, and collecting perishable evidence.

**Follow-up investigation** — Investigative activity conducted after the preliminary investigation to identify suspects, gather additional evidence, and prepare a case for prosecution.

**Solvability factors** — Information that increases the likelihood of solving a case, such as a named suspect, a witness, physical evidence, or video recordings.

**Case status** — The current classification of a case, such as open-active, open-inactive, cleared by arrest, exceptionally cleared, or unfounded.

## 600.3 Preliminary Investigations
The first responding deputy is responsible for the preliminary investigation unless relieved by a supervisor or investigator. The deputy shall:

- Provide aid to injured persons and secure the scene.
- Identify and separate victims, witnesses, and suspects.
- Obtain initial statements and identify any video or digital evidence.
- Protect and collect evidence under Policy 802.
- Request specialized resources, such as crime scene technicians or investigators, when needed.
- Complete a report under Policy 602 before the end of shift.

## 600.4 Investigator Call-Out
A supervisor shall request an on-call investigator for homicides, suspicious deaths, sexual assaults, robberies with injury, shootings, child abuse with serious injury, kidnappings, officer-involved critical incidents, and other major incidents. Once the investigator arrives, the investigator assumes responsibility for the investigation, and patrol members shall provide assistance as directed.

## 600.5 Case Assignment
The Investigations Division Lieutenant or designee shall review reports daily and assign cases based on seriousness, solvability factors, and investigator workload. Cases with no solvability factors may be classified as open-inactive with written notice to the victim and may be reactivated when new information develops. Persons crimes, cases involving vulnerable victims, and cases with named suspects shall receive priority.

## 600.6 Conducting Investigations
Investigators shall pursue all reasonable leads, including those that may point away from an identified suspect. Investigations shall be conducted without bias based on race, ethnicity, religion, gender, sexual orientation, disability, or socioeconomic status. Investigators shall record custodial interviews of suspects in felony cases in their entirety and should record interviews of key witnesses.

Information obtained from databases, informants, or analytical tools, including any decision-support tool governed by Policy 830, shall be treated as an investigative lead that must be independently corroborated. Such information shall not be the sole basis for an arrest, search warrant, or charging decision.

## 600.7 Case File and Documentation
Each assigned case shall have a case file in the records management system containing all reports, supplemental reports, evidence records, photographs, recordings, warrants, and correspondence. Investigators shall document investigative activity in supplemental reports at least every 30 days while the case is active. Notes and drafts that contain substantive investigative information may be subject to discovery and shall be preserved consistent with Office procedures.

## 600.8 Brady and Disclosure Obligations
Investigators shall preserve and disclose to the prosecuting attorney all information that may be exculpatory or that may impeach a witness, including information about members' credibility, regardless of whether the investigator believes the information is significant. Investigators shall not withhold, alter, or destroy evidence or notes. Questions about disclosure obligations shall be directed to the prosecuting attorney.

## 600.9 Victim Communication
Investigators shall contact the victim within five business days of case assignment, provide the investigator's contact information, and explain the investigative process. Victims shall be updated when significant developments occur, including an arrest, a charging decision, or a change in case status. Victims of violent crimes shall be informed of available victim services and their rights under applicable state law.

## 600.10 Case Review and Closure
Supervisors shall review each active case at least every 30 days to assess progress and direct additional steps. A case may be closed as cleared by arrest, exceptionally cleared when an arrest cannot be made for reasons outside the Office's control, or unfounded when the investigation establishes no crime occurred. Closure requires supervisor approval, and the reason shall be documented in the case file.

## 600.11 Cold Case Review
Unsolved homicides and sexual assaults shall be reviewed at least every three years, or when new forensic technology becomes available, to determine whether additional investigation is warranted.
