# Policy 302 — Duty to Intercede and Report

> Kestrel County Sheriff's Office Policy Manual · Effective 2023-09-01 · Revised 2025-11-10
> FICTIONAL POLICY FOR DEMONSTRATION PURPOSES ONLY

## 302.1 Purpose and Scope
This policy establishes the affirmative obligation of every Kestrel County Sheriff's Office member to step in when another member, or an officer from any agency, is using excessive force or engaging in other clear misconduct, and to report what was observed. The Office expects members to protect the public and one another from conduct that violates the law or this manual, and recognizes that early intervention prevents injuries, protects careers, and preserves public trust.

This policy applies to all sworn, detention, reserve, and civilian members while on duty or while acting under the authority of the Office, including during joint operations, task forces, and mutual-aid responses. It supplements Section 300.5 of Policy 300 (Use of Force).

## 302.2 Definitions
**Intercede** — To take verbal or physical action intended to prevent or stop misconduct by another officer, including giving a direct instruction to stop, physically separating the officer from the subject, or summoning a supervisor.

**Excessive force** — Force that is clearly beyond what is objectively reasonable under the totality of the circumstances, as described in Policy 300.

**Misconduct** — Conduct that is illegal, abusive, or a serious violation of Office policy, including excessive force, unlawful searches or arrests, theft, falsification of records, and discriminatory treatment.

**Retaliation** — Any adverse action, threat, harassment, exclusion, or intimidation directed at a member because the member interceded, reported misconduct, or cooperated with an investigation.

## 302.3 Duty to Intercede
A member who observes another officer using force that the member reasonably believes is clearly beyond what is objectively reasonable shall intercede when in a position to do so safely. This duty applies regardless of the rank, seniority, assignment, or agency of the officer using force.

Intervention should be the least intrusive action that will be effective. Members should begin with clear verbal instruction, such as calling the officer by name and telling them to stop or to step back, and escalate to physical intervention only when verbal intervention is ineffective or there is no time for it. Members are also expected to intercede when they observe an officer about to use force that appears unjustified, such as striking a restrained subject, or when they observe a subject showing signs of medical distress that are being ignored.

A member is not required to intercede in a way that would place the member or others at unreasonable risk of serious injury, but the member must still report the conduct.

## 302.4 Other Misconduct
The duty to intercede is not limited to force. Members who observe another officer engaging in conduct that is clearly unlawful or a serious violation of policy, such as an unlawful search, planting or destroying evidence, making false statements, or treating a person with discriminatory hostility, shall take reasonable steps to stop the conduct and shall report it.

Members who are asked or directed to take part in conduct they believe is unlawful shall decline and notify a supervisor. Following an order is not a defense to participating in clearly unlawful conduct.

## 302.5 Reporting Requirements
A member who interceded, or who observed conduct requiring intervention, shall notify an on-duty supervisor as soon as practical and no later than the end of the member's shift. If the supervisor was involved in the conduct, the member shall notify the next higher-ranking supervisor or the Professional Standards Unit directly.

The member shall complete a written memorandum or supplemental report describing:

- The date, time, location, and case number, if any.
- The officers and subjects involved and any witnesses.
- The specific conduct observed, described in factual terms.
- The action the member took to intercede, and the response.
- Any injuries and medical care provided.

Reports shall be factual and complete. Members shall not omit information, delay reporting to protect another officer, or coordinate their accounts with other witnesses.

## 302.6 Supervisor Responsibilities
A supervisor who receives a report of intervention or misconduct shall promptly ensure the safety and medical care of any affected person, preserve evidence including body-worn camera recordings, and notify the District Commander. The supervisor shall forward all related reports to the Professional Standards Unit within 24 hours.

When the report alleges criminal conduct, the supervisor shall consult the on-call command officer about whether the involved officer should be relieved of enforcement duties pending review. Supervisors shall document that the reporting member was advised of the protections in Section 302.7.

## 302.7 Protection From Retaliation
Retaliation against a member who interceded, reported misconduct, or participated in an investigation is prohibited and is itself serious misconduct subject to discipline up to and including termination. Members who believe they have been subjected to retaliation may report it to any supervisor, the Professional Standards Unit, or the Office of the Sheriff.

Supervisors who become aware of retaliation, or of conduct that could reasonably be perceived as retaliation, shall take immediate steps to stop it and report it. The Office will consider a member's good-faith intervention as a positive factor in performance evaluations.

## 302.8 Failure to Intercede or Report
A member who fails to intercede when required, or who fails to report observed misconduct, may be subject to discipline even if the member did not personally use force or commit the underlying misconduct. In reviewing such cases, the Office will consider whether the member had a realistic opportunity to intervene, the member's proximity and awareness, and the time available.

## 302.9 Training
All members shall receive training on the duty to intercede during initial academy or field training and at least every two years thereafter. Training shall include practical scenarios on recognizing escalating behavior in a partner, verbal intervention techniques, and peer support strategies that help members accept intervention without resentment.
