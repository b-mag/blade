# Policy 330 — Crisis Intervention and Mental Health Response

> Kestrel County Sheriff's Office Policy Manual · Effective 2024-02-01 · Revised 2026-05-20
> FICTIONAL POLICY FOR DEMONSTRATION PURPOSES ONLY

## 330.1 Purpose and Scope
Deputies of the Kestrel County Sheriff's Office regularly encounter people experiencing a mental-health crisis, a developmental disability, or a substance-related emergency. This policy guides members in recognizing these situations, responding with appropriate resources, and resolving encounters safely and humanely. The Office's goal is to connect people in crisis with treatment rather than the criminal justice system whenever public safety allows, while protecting the safety of the person, the public, and deputies.

## 330.2 Definitions
**Person in crisis** — A person whose behavior, thinking, or emotional state, due to mental illness, substance use, developmental disability, or acute stress, impairs their ability to cope and may place themselves or others at risk.

**Crisis Intervention Team (CIT) deputy** — A deputy who has completed the Office's 40-hour crisis intervention course and is designated to respond to crisis calls.

**Co-responder** — A licensed mental-health clinician from the Kestrel County Behavioral Health Department who is paired with a deputy to respond to crisis calls.

**Emergency detention** — The involuntary transport of a person for a mental-health evaluation when authorized by applicable state law.

## 330.3 Recognizing a Person in Crisis
Deputies should be alert to signs that a person may be in crisis, including confused or disorganized speech, hallucinations or paranoia, extreme agitation or withdrawal, statements about suicide or self-harm, inappropriate dress for conditions, or behavior that does not fit the situation. Deputies should also consider that conditions such as diabetes, head injury, autism, deafness, or dementia may present similarly. Recognizing these signs early helps deputies choose an approach that reduces the chance of escalation.

## 330.4 CIT Deputies and Co-Responders
When a call involves a person believed to be in crisis, dispatch shall assign a CIT deputy when one is available, and shall request the co-responder team during its hours of service in each district. Non-CIT deputies who arrive first should stabilize the scene and wait for CIT or co-responder support when time and safety allow.

The co-responder clinician may assess the person, provide on-scene counseling, and help arrange voluntary treatment or a referral. Deputies remain responsible for scene safety, and the clinician shall not be placed in a position of danger. When a scene involves weapons or violence, the clinician shall remain at a safe staging location until deputies determine the scene is secure.

## 330.5 De-Escalation With Persons in Crisis
When safe and feasible, deputies shall use de-escalation techniques consistent with Policy 300, including:

- Slowing down and allowing time, as a crisis often lessens with time.
- Reducing stimulation, such as turning off sirens and lights and limiting the number of deputies who speak.
- Using a calm tone, simple language, and active listening.
- Introducing themselves by name and expressing a desire to help.
- Avoiding threats, arguments, or challenging delusions directly.
- Maintaining a safe distance and a non-threatening posture.

Deputies should recognize that a person in crisis may not be able to comply with commands quickly, and non-compliance alone is not a basis for force.

## 330.6 Persons in Crisis With Weapons
When a person in crisis is armed, deputies shall follow Section 300.3.6 of Policy 300 for edged weapons and the general deadly force standards of Section 300.4. Deputies should establish a perimeter, use cover and distance, request a supervisor, CIT deputy, and less-lethal options, and avoid closing distance unnecessarily.

When the person is a danger only to themselves and is contained, deputies should generally prioritize time and communication over forced entry or confrontation. A supervisor shall manage the scene and shall consider requesting the crisis negotiation team and tactical resources when the incident is prolonged.

## 330.7 Emergency Detention
A deputy may take a person into protective custody for an emergency mental-health evaluation when the deputy has probable cause to believe that, as a result of mental illness, the person presents a danger to themselves or others, or is unable to care for their basic needs, as defined by applicable state law. The deputy shall complete the required emergency detention application, transport the person to an approved receiving facility, and remain until custody is transferred.

Deputies should encourage voluntary treatment when possible. Emergency detention is a civil process, not an arrest, and the person shall be transported in a manner that is safe and dignified, using restraints only as needed under Policy 306.

## 330.8 Firearms and Dangerous Weapons
When a person is taken into custody for an emergency detention, deputies may take temporary custody of firearms in the person's possession or plain view for safekeeping, consistent with applicable state law. Deputies should inform the person and family of the process for return and of any available extreme risk protection order process. Firearms shall be handled under Policy 802.

## 330.9 Documentation and Follow-Up
Deputies shall complete a report for all crisis calls that result in a detention, use of force, referral, or significant contact, and shall flag the call as crisis-related for data collection. Reports shall describe observed behaviors in objective terms and should avoid diagnostic labels unless known from a reliable source. The CIT Coordinator shall review crisis-related reports to identify repeat contacts that may benefit from co-responder follow-up.

## 330.10 Training
All sworn deputies shall receive at least eight hours of crisis intervention training during initial training and refresher training every two years. The Office aims to train at least 25 percent of patrol deputies in each district as CIT deputies.
