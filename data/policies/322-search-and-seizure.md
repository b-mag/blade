# Policy 322 — Search and Seizure

> Kestrel County Sheriff's Office Policy Manual · Effective 2023-04-15 · Revised 2025-10-01
> FICTIONAL POLICY FOR DEMONSTRATION PURPOSES ONLY

## 322.1 Purpose and Scope
The Fourth Amendment to the United States Constitution protects people from unreasonable searches and seizures. This policy provides general guidance to members of the Kestrel County Sheriff's Office on conducting lawful searches and seizures and on documenting the legal basis for them. Because search and seizure law changes through court decisions, members are expected to stay current through Office training and legal updates and to consult a supervisor or the prosecuting attorney when uncertain.

## 322.2 Definitions
**Search** — A governmental intrusion into an area where a person has a reasonable expectation of privacy, or a physical intrusion onto a protected area to obtain information.

**Seizure of a person** — A restraint of a person's freedom of movement by physical force or show of authority to which the person submits.

**Seizure of property** — A meaningful interference with a person's possessory interest in property.

**Reasonable suspicion** — Specific, articulable facts that, together with rational inferences, would lead a reasonable deputy to suspect that criminal activity is afoot.

**Probable cause** — Facts and circumstances sufficient to lead a reasonable person to believe that a crime has been committed and that the person committed it, or that evidence of a crime will be found in a particular place.

## 322.3 Search Warrants
Searches should be conducted under a warrant whenever practical. Deputies preparing an affidavit shall describe the facts establishing probable cause accurately and completely and shall not include information they know or suspect to be false. The place to be searched and items to be seized shall be described with particularity.

Before serving a warrant, the deputy shall brief a supervisor, complete a risk assessment, and conduct a pre-service briefing with all participating members. Deputies shall knock and announce their presence and purpose unless the warrant authorizes otherwise or exigent circumstances exist. A copy of the warrant and an inventory of items seized shall be left at the premises, and the return shall be filed with the court as required by applicable state law.

## 322.4 Warrantless Searches
Warrantless searches are presumed unreasonable unless an established exception applies. Common exceptions include:

- **Consent** — Voluntarily given by a person with authority over the area.
- **Search incident to arrest** — Of the arrestee and the area within immediate control at the time of arrest.
- **Exigent circumstances** — When there is an immediate need to prevent harm, destruction of evidence, or escape.
- **Automobile exception** — When there is probable cause to believe a vehicle contains evidence of a crime.
- **Plain view** — When an item is lawfully observed and its incriminating nature is immediately apparent.
- **Inventory** — Of lawfully impounded vehicles under standardized Office procedures.

Deputies shall articulate in their reports the specific exception relied upon and the facts supporting it.

## 322.5 Consent Searches
Consent must be voluntary and not the result of coercion or a claim of authority the deputy does not have. Deputies should advise the person that they may refuse or limit consent and may withdraw it at any time. Written consent on the Office form, or a body-worn camera recording of verbal consent, is preferred. The scope of the search shall not exceed the consent given. When multiple occupants are present and one objects, deputies should consult a supervisor before proceeding.

## 322.6 Detentions and Frisks
A deputy may briefly detain a person based on reasonable suspicion. The detention shall last no longer than necessary to confirm or dispel the suspicion. A pat-down for weapons is permitted only when the deputy reasonably believes the person may be armed and dangerous; the frisk is limited to the outer clothing for weapons, and the reasons shall be documented. A detention may not be prolonged for unrelated investigation without additional reasonable suspicion.

## 322.7 Searches of Persons
Searches of persons should be conducted, when practical, by a member of the same sex as the person being searched, unless the circumstances present an immediate risk. Deputies shall respect the gender identity of the person being searched consistent with Office procedures. Strip searches and body cavity searches are governed by Detention Center procedures and applicable state law, require supervisor approval, and shall never be conducted in the field. Body cavity searches may be performed only by medical personnel under a warrant.

## 322.8 Digital Devices
Searching the contents of a cell phone or other digital device generally requires a warrant, even when the device is seized incident to arrest. Deputies may seize and secure a device pending a warrant when they have probable cause that it contains evidence. Devices should be placed in airplane mode or a signal-blocking bag to preserve data and handled under Policy 802 (Property and Evidence).

## 322.9 Documentation and Supervisory Review
All searches, whether or not evidence is found, shall be documented in the incident or arrest report, including the legal basis, scope, members involved, and items seized. Supervisors shall review reports for compliance with this policy and address deficiencies through training or referral. Members shall not use race, ethnicity, religion, or other protected characteristics as a basis for any stop, search, or seizure, except as part of a specific suspect description.
