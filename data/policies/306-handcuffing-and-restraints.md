# Policy 306 — Handcuffing and Restraints

> Kestrel County Sheriff's Office Policy Manual · Effective 2023-06-01 · Revised 2025-08-20
> FICTIONAL POLICY FOR DEMONSTRATION PURPOSES ONLY

## 306.1 Purpose and Scope
This policy provides guidelines for the use of handcuffs and other restraint devices by members of the Kestrel County Sheriff's Office. Restraints are used to protect deputies, the public, and the restrained person, and to prevent escape. They must be applied in a way that is safe, humane, and consistent with training. Restraint of a person who does not resist is not generally a reportable use of force, but any force used to apply restraints is governed by Policy 300 (Use of Force).

## 306.2 Definitions
**Handcuffs** — Office-issued or approved metal hinged or chain handcuffs, or disposable flex cuffs.

**Leg restraints** — Ankle cuffs or approved hobble straps used to limit movement of the legs.

**Restraint chair** — A padded chair with integrated straps used in the Kestrel County Detention Center to restrain a person who presents an immediate risk of harm.

**Spit hood** — A breathable mesh covering placed over a person's head to prevent the transmission of bodily fluids.

**Positional asphyxia** — A condition in which a person's body position prevents adequate breathing, which can lead to death.

## 306.3 Use of Handcuffs
Persons under arrest for a criminal offense should be handcuffed, unless the deputy reasonably determines that handcuffs are unnecessary because of the person's age, physical condition, or other factors and the person does not present a risk of escape or harm. A deputy's decision not to handcuff an arrestee should be documented.

Handcuffs may also be used during an investigative detention when the deputy reasonably believes it is necessary for safety or to prevent flight, such as when the person is suspected of a violent offense or is acting aggressively. When used during a detention, handcuffs should be removed as soon as the need for them ends, and the reason for their use shall be documented.

Handcuffs should be applied with the person's hands behind the back, palms facing outward. Deputies shall check for proper fit and double-lock the handcuffs as soon as practical to prevent tightening and injury.

## 306.4 Special Circumstances
Deputies should use discretion and consider alternatives when restraining:

- **Pregnant persons** — Restraints should be applied in front of the body when safe, and leg restraints, waist chains, and abdominal pressure shall be avoided.
- **Juveniles** — Juveniles may be restrained under the same safety standards as adults, consistent with Policy 324 (Juveniles), but should not be restrained to a fixed object.
- **Persons with disabilities or injuries** — Restraint methods should be adapted, such as using two sets of handcuffs linked together or front cuffing, to avoid aggravating an injury.
- **Elderly persons** — Deputies should consider the person's fragility and actual risk.

Persons shall not be handcuffed to a vehicle, pole, or other fixed object except in an emergency and under constant observation.

## 306.5 Leg Restraints and Hobbles
Leg restraints may be used when a handcuffed person continues to kick, damage property, or attempt to escape, or when transporting a person who presents a known escape risk. Hobble straps shall not be used to connect a person's ankles to their handcuffs behind the back (sometimes called a hogtie or maximal restraint), which is prohibited.

After leg restraints are applied, the person shall be placed in a seated or side recovery position and shall not be left face-down. The deputy applying leg restraints shall notify a supervisor and document the use in the arrest report.

## 306.6 Positional Asphyxia Prevention
Restrained persons are at increased risk of positional asphyxia, especially when they are obese, intoxicated, in a mental-health crisis, or have been involved in a prolonged struggle. Deputies shall:

- Move a restrained person out of the prone position as soon as control is achieved.
- Avoid placing body weight on the person's back, chest, or neck.
- Continuously monitor breathing, speech, skin color, and level of consciousness.
- Treat any statement such as "I can't breathe" as a potential medical emergency and immediately reassess the person's position and condition.
- Request emergency medical services for signs of respiratory distress or unresponsiveness.

## 306.7 Spit Hoods
A spit hood may be used on a restrained person who is spitting or biting, or who has threatened to do so. Only Office-approved, breathable hoods shall be used. A spit hood shall not be used on a person who is vomiting, bleeding heavily from the face, unconscious, or showing signs of respiratory distress. A person wearing a spit hood shall be continuously observed and the hood removed as soon as the threat ends or the person is placed in a secure setting.

## 306.8 Transport of Restrained Persons
Restrained persons shall be transported seated and secured with a seat belt whenever possible. Deputies shall not transport a restrained person face-down. Deputies shall keep the person under observation during transport and shall check the condition of the person and the fit of restraints upon arrival. Members of the opposite sex, juveniles, and adults shall be transported according to the separation requirements in transport procedures.

## 306.9 Documentation
Deputies shall document in the arrest or incident report the type of restraints used, the reason for any use of restraints during a detention, any use of leg restraints or spit hoods, any injury or complaint related to restraints, and the person's condition at the time of transfer. Any force used beyond routine handcuffing of a compliant person shall be reported under Policy 300.
